01 / ABOUT

About me

I am a Postdoctoral Associate in the group of Prof. Javad Shabani at the Center for Quantum Information Physics, New York University. My research is on hybrid superconducting–semiconducting quantum systems, superconducting qubits and circuit quantum electrodynamics, and mesoscopic quantum transport.

Before NYU, I was a Postdoctoral Associate with Prof. Vladimir Manucharyan at the University of Maryland, College Park (2022–2025), where I developed strongly anharmonic, flux-tunable transmons in InAs–Al two-dimensional heterostructures.

I received my Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Basel, Switzerland, with Prof. Christian Schönenberger. My thesis was on spin projection and correlation experiments in nanoelectronic devices, and it included spin cross-correlation experiments in a Cooper pair splitter (Nature, 2022). Before that, I earned my B.S.–M.S. in Physical Sciences at IISER Kolkata.

      03 / RESEARCH

      Research

      I use hybrid superconductor–semiconductor materials to build quantum circuits whose properties can be tuned and engineered. My work spans qubit design, mesoscopic transport, and materials development.

      Hybrid superconducting qubits

      Flux-tunable, strongly anharmonic transmons and gatemon-type qubits built from InAs–Al 2D heterostructures. I use RF spectroscopy of Andreev bound states and Fraunhofer-modulated modes in Josephson weak links.

      Spin correlations & Cooper pair splitting

      Spin-resolved current cross-correlations in Cooper pair splitters built from InAs nanowire double quantum dots. I developed a double quantum dot spin valve to detect spin projections, a step toward detecting entanglement in solid-state electron entanglers.

      Josephson arrays & quantum materials

      Quantum phase transitions in Josephson junction arrays (e.g. super-honeycomb lattices), crystalline group-IV Josephson junctions, and low-loss superconducting resonators on III–V substrates.
